# ADR 016 - Readers are supporters with a session; access to content is enforced server-side

- **Status**: accepted
- **Date**: 2026-10-21
- **Builds on**: ADR 003 (supporter identity), ADR 005 rule 7 (membership as subscription kind), ADR 012 (articles)

## Context

Journalistic tenants need members-only and paid content; campaign tenants need member areas (Republiek). Substack and Ghost prove the shape: readers identify by e-mail with a magic link, no password; a post is free, for members, or for a paid tier; a teaser is shown to everyone else. The mistake to avoid is a client-side paywall (the body is in the HTML and a stylesheet hides it) and a second identity model next to supporters.

## Decision

1. **A reader is a supporter (ADR 003) with a reader session.** Readers sign in with a magic link sent to their e-mail; the session is a signed, HttpOnly, SameSite cookie bound to the tenant hostname (ADR 014). No passwords; WebAuthn may be added later for the same identity. Signing in is itself a supporter event (`reader.signed_in`), so the projection knows active readers.
2. **Access tiers are tenant data** (`access_tiers`: slug, localised name, description, order; `kind: free | paid`). A paid tier is backed by a subscription with `kind: membership` and `tierId` (ADR 005). Comped and gifted access are supporter events (`access.granted` with tier, reason, `until`), so an operator can grant a journalist or a donor access without a payment.
3. **Content access is a field on the item** (ADR 012): `access: { level: public | members | tier, tierIds?, teaserBlocks?: number }`. The projection answers `reader.hasAccess(item)`; the renderer and the read route (ADR 012 rule 7) never serialise restricted blocks for a reader without access. What they return instead is the teaser (the first `teaserBlocks` blocks, default 2) plus a subscribe or sign-in prompt block generated by the renderer. The same rule applies to feeds, search and the Markdown renderer.
4. **Paywall modes in v1**: hard (teaser only), tier-based, and comp. **Later, each a registry entry, not a model change**: metered (n free items per month, counted in a first-party functional cookie plus the reader session, no consent banner needed), time-based (free after or before a date), share links (a signed URL that grants one item for a limited time), section-level defaults.
5. **Search engines get the truth**: restricted articles emit `NewsArticle` with `isAccessibleForFree: false` and `hasPart` marking the paid section; no cloaking. Full-text feeds for paying readers are per-reader signed feed URLs (later).
6. **Reader self-service reuses donor self-service** (ADR 005 rule 6): one page, signed link or reader session, showing subscriptions, access, newsletters and consent, with change, pause and cancel.
7. **Comments, likes and recommendations are not in the model.** A discussion feature is a later ADR if a tenant needs it; it carries moderation duties this project does not take on by default.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Separate `readers` table with passwords**: two identities per person (supporter and reader), the exact drift the registry exists to prevent; passwords are a liability no magic-link product needs.
- **Client-side paywall** (body in HTML, overlay): bypassed by reader mode and view-source, and it leaks paid content to crawlers.
- **Stripe-style paywall via a third party (Memberful, Steady)**: another system of record for members and money; ADR 005 already owns the ledger.

## Consequences

- Access checks live in `core` (`content.access` module) and are the first thing the renderer and the read route call; a block conformance test asserts that a restricted fixture never appears in output for an anonymous principal.
- `Principal` gets a `reader` actor kind next to `operator`, `api_key`, `agent` and `system`.
- Fase order: tiers and `access` field land with ADR 012 (fase 1, hard paywall against comp grants works without payments); paid tiers follow Mollie in fase 3; metered and share links in fase 5.

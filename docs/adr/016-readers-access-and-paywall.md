# ADR 016 - Readers are supporters with a session; access to content is enforced server-side

- **Status**: accepted
- **Date**: 2026-10-21
- **Builds on**: ADR 003 (supporter identity), ADR 005 rule 7 (membership as subscription kind), ADR 012 (articles)
- **Amended**: 2026-10-21 by ADR 018 (rule 3: explicit teaser replaces `teaserBlocks`; access applies to every representation; access-aware caching) and ADR 019 (session bound to `supporter_id`; sign-in retention)
- **Paid-tier ownership refined**: 2026-10-24 by [ADR 024](024-managed-integrations-and-external-ownership.md). Rule 2's mandatory Mycel membership-subscription backing and the rejected alternative's blanket refusal of an external membership system are superseded only for an explicitly externally owned subscription. Mycel still enforces access server-side from verified, tenant-scoped entitlement status; the client-side paywall rejection remains in force. Rule 6 self-service exposes only operations the configured owner supports.

## Context

Journalistic tenants need members-only and paid content; campaign tenants need member areas (Republiek). Substack and Ghost prove the shape: readers identify by e-mail with a magic link, no password; a post is free, for members, or for a paid tier; a teaser is shown to everyone else. The mistake to avoid is a client-side paywall (the body is in the HTML and a stylesheet hides it) and a second identity model next to supporters.

## Decision

1. **A reader is a supporter (ADR 003) with a reader session.** Readers sign in with a magic link sent to their e-mail; the session is a signed, HttpOnly, SameSite cookie bound to the tenant hostname (ADR 014). No passwords; WebAuthn may be added later for the same identity. Signing in is itself a supporter event (`reader.signed_in`), so the projection knows active readers.
2. **Access tiers are tenant data** (`access_tiers`: slug, localised name, description, order; `kind: free | paid`). A paid tier is backed by a subscription with `kind: membership` and `tierId` (ADR 005). Comped and gifted access are supporter events (`access.granted` with tier, reason, `until`), so an operator can grant a journalist or a donor access without a payment.
3. **Content access is a field on the item** (ADR 012): `access: { level: public | members | tier, tierIds?, teaser: lead | marker | { blocks: n } }` (ADR 018 rule 6). The projection answers `reader.hasAccess(item)`; the renderer and the read route (ADR 012 rule 7) never serialise restricted blocks for a reader without access. What they return instead is the teaser (ADR 018 rule 6; default the lead only) plus a subscribe or sign-in prompt block generated by the renderer. The same rule applies to feeds, search and the Markdown renderer.
4. **Paywall modes in v1**: hard (teaser only), tier-based, and comp. **Later, each a registry entry, not a model change**: metered (n free items per month, counted in a first-party functional cookie plus the reader session, no consent banner needed), time-based (free after or before a date), share links (a signed URL that grants one item for a limited time), section-level defaults.
5. **Search engines get the truth**: restricted articles emit `NewsArticle` with `isAccessibleForFree: false` and `hasPart` marking the paid section; no cloaking. Full-text feeds for paying readers are per-reader signed feed URLs (later).
6. **Reader self-service reuses donor self-service** (ADR 005 rule 6): one page, signed link or reader session, showing subscriptions, access, newsletters and consent, with change, pause and cancel.
7. **Comments, likes and recommendations are not in the model.** A discussion feature is a later ADR if a tenant needs it; it carries moderation duties this project does not take on by default.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Separate `readers` table with passwords**: two identities per person (supporter and reader), the exact drift the registry exists to prevent; passwords are a liability no magic-link product needs.
- **Client-side paywall** (body in HTML, overlay): bypassed by reader mode and view-source, and it leaks paid content to crawlers.
- **Stripe-style paywall via a third party (Memberful, Steady)**: another system of record for members and money; ADR 005 already owns the ledger.

## Consequences

- Access checks live in `core` (`content.access` module) and are the first thing the renderer and the read route call; a block conformance test asserts that a restricted fixture never appears in output for an anonymous principal.
- `Principal` gets a `reader` actor kind next to `operator`, `api_key`, `agent` and `system`.
- Fase order: tiers and `access` field land with ADR 012 (fase 1, hard paywall against comp grants works without payments); paid tiers follow Mollie in fase 3; metered and share links in fase 5.
- 2026-11-16 (migration 0068), rule 2 in operation: `access_tiers` also carries a stable id that `tierIds` and grants point at; slug and kind are fixed at create and a tier is never deleted, so no tier write widens published access. A grant is not a table: `access.granted` (tier, `comp` or `gift`, reason, optional exclusive `until`) and `access.revoked` are supporter events, and the projection keeps one standing grant per tier, a later grant replacing the earlier one. Writing a tier asks for `content:publish`; granting and revoking ask for `tenant:admin`, the same over HTTP and MCP. `hasAccess(reader, item, now)` is pure and compares `until` at each read (ADR 019 rule 4). An item's `tierIds` must be tiers of its tenant on every save and at publication. Rejected: a grants table beside the log (two sources of truth for an erasure and a re-fold to keep in step) and a tier delete with a reference check (a check per representation for an operation nobody needs yet).
- 2026-11-17 (migration 0069), rule 1 in operation: the link is a deterministic signed payload of tenant, link row and expiry, good once for 15 minutes; the row keeps the address and a hash of the token, and the outbox event carries the row's id only, so the mail is rebuilt from the row and no token or address sits in a queue. The link opens a page whose button posts; only that post signs in. The session is a row (`reader_sessions`) named by a host-only, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax` cookie that ends 12 hours after sign-in; the supporter is read from the row and the live projection on every request, so sign-out and an erasure end it at once. The `reader` actor carries a session id and the fixed capabilities `content:read` and `media:read`, and reaches only the visitor's use cases and its own sign-out; the sign-in use cases admit no key, agent or operator. Asking for a link answers the same for every address and makes no supporter; one mail per address per day. Contract and constants: `docs/reference/readers.md`. Rejected: a stateless session like the operator's (nothing to end before its clock, which an erasure needs), signing in on the GET of the link (a mail scanner would sign in and use the link), and a random token stored only as a hash (the mail could not be rebuilt for a reconcile without keeping the token).
- 2026-11-17, rule 3 in operation: `content.published.get` makes the decision once, on the pinned revision, and projects either the whole document or the teaser document, which is the same document with the body cut before a locale, a description or a reference is derived from it. The teaser keeps the document header (title, lead, hero image) and the explicit search and card fields; it drops the blocks behind the boundary, an article's corrections and an event's when and where. The answer carries `gate`, `signin` or `tier`, and the front writes the prompt. A restricted address is read as a visitor first and as the reader only when the request carries the cookie, so a public page never reads one; a reader's access opens the item's body and nothing around it, and references, cards and media stay a visitor's. The JSON read route has no reader. Rule 5's `hasPart` names the reader gate, the element that stands where the paid part is for everyone who is not let in. Rejected: a flag the renderer honours with the body in the projection (one forgetful representation leaks it), resolving the reader on every page (a session read per public request, and public pages would vary on the cookie), and a reader credential for the read route (a second reader identity beside the session).
- 2026-11-17, rule 3 for search (#42 B): the site search indexes a restricted revision with two token sets, those of its teaser document and all of them, plus its level and tiers. A visitor and a reader without access match the teaser tokens only and get the teaser's title and excerpt with the hit marked `restricted`; a reader with access matches the whole text, the tiers they stand in being computed at the read. The use case decides a second time with `hasAccess` on the revision it reads, and a reader's results page is `private, no-store`. Rejected: leaving restricted items out of search (a reader cannot find what they signed in for) and filtering one token set after the query (a page of hits would shrink and its next link would tell what words a body holds).
- 2026-11-18 (migration 0079), rule 2 for paid tiers (MY-254 PR 4): a paid tier carries its prices per interval and a `priceVersion` that a membership records; each paid period of a membership writes `access.granted` with a third kind, `paid`, until `paidThrough` plus the dunning grace, so `hasAccess` keeps one source and an ended or cancelled membership closes when that grant's `until` passes. An operator gives only `comp` and `gift`. The gate carries `join`, the signup page of a tier that sells a membership. A payment never signs anyone in: the return page of a membership mails the ordinary sign-in link to the address that paid. Contract: `docs/reference/donations.md` § Memberships. Rejected: revoking the grant when a membership ends (the member paid for the rest of the period) and `hasAccess` reading subscriptions (a second source for one decision).
